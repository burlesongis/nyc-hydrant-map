About this map

An interactive look at how densely fire hydrants are spread across New York City's residential neighborhoods.

The idea

Count hydrants by neighborhood and Staten Island wins: its large neighborhoods hold the most hydrants. Divide by area, though, and the story flips. Manhattan's small, dense neighborhoods have the highest density, led by Gramercy at 384.7 hydrants per km².

The map lets you see both views. Use the Color by menu to switch between density and raw counts, and tick Show individual hydrants and zoom in to see every hydrant location.

Both NYC datasets were downloaded in September 2026 through the NYC Open Data API.

Methodology

  1. Load. The hydrant points and neighborhood polygons were loaded into a PostGIS database.
  2. Filter to residential neighborhoods. NTAs carry a type code; type 0 marks residential neighborhoods. Parks, airports, cemeteries and similar areas are left out, leaving 197 neighborhoods.
  3. Count hydrants. A spatial join (ST_Contains) assigns each hydrant to the neighborhood polygon it falls inside. Of the 109,725 hydrants, 108,121 fall inside a residential neighborhood.
  4. Compute density. Hydrants per km² is the hydrant count divided by the neighborhood's area in square kilometers, calculated on the geography type so the area is measured on the earth's surface.
  5. Build vector tiles. The neighborhoods and the hydrant points were converted to vector tiles with tippecanoe and packed into two PMTiles files. The browser downloads only the tiles it needs using HTTP range requests, so no tile server is involved.
  6. Render. MapLibre GL JS draws the tiles and colors each neighborhood into five bins. The map colors and the legend are generated from the same settings, so they always agree.

Things to keep in mind

  • Neighborhoods are statistical areas. NTAs are built by the city for reporting. They do not always match the boundaries people use in everyday life.
  • Boundary cases. A hydrant sitting exactly on a neighborhood edge is not counted by ST_Contains.
  • Color bins are fixed. Density uses breaks at 100, 150, 200 and 300 hydrants per km², and counts use 300, 450, 600 and 800. Different breaks would change how the map looks.
  • Zoomed-out hydrant view is a sample. To keep the tiles light, tippecanoe thins hydrant points at lower zoom levels. Zoom in fully to see every hydrant.
  • Locations only. The data shows where hydrants are, not whether they work or how much water they deliver.
